# Audio capture from a stream: rewind, do not record

## Thesis

mpv is already keeping the whole past of the current stream in RAM, under the daemon's exact current options, with no config change: `seekable-ranges` starts at 0.0 and `bof-cached=true` on Icecast mp3, Icecast AAC and HLS. So the ring the mark-gesture design said was missing already exists, it holds compressed packets at 25-32 KB/s instead of PCM at 176 KB/s, and `dump-cache <start> <end> <file>` hands back an arbitrary past window as a bitstream copy at ~350 MB/s with zero network. That inverts the whole feature: capture is not something you start, it is something you stop. Nothing is armed, nothing is recorded, nothing touches disk until he presses - and the press is allowed to be late, because the cut is taken backwards. My brief was to build on `stream-record`; the honest finding is that `stream-record` is the wrong primitive for this and I am demoting it. mpv's own manual says of a runtime-set `stream-record`: "this will write only data that is appended at the end of the cache, and the already cached data cannot be written. You can try the dump-cache command as an alternative." A press-armed `stream-record` is exactly as structurally late as the press. It keeps exactly one narrow job (below), and `dump-cache` is the spine.

## The tap

One tap, and it is the one already open: mpv's demuxer/stream cache for the currently playing entry.

Verified about it: `--record-file` does not exist in mpv 0.41.0 (`option not found`). `--stream-record` exists as option and runtime property and does a true bitstream copy (16 s of NTS mp3 -> 476,642 B, ffprobe `bit_rate=256540` against a 256,000 source), but it is prospective-only, is flushed in 256 KiB blocks so the file is invalid until disarm, silently does not follow a `loadfile ... replace` while still reading back the armed path, and corrupts the file outright on a format change (mp3-armed + AAC replace -> `Failed to find two consecutive MPEG audio frames`). `dump-cache` is retroactive, bounded, synchronous and effectively free: 42.5 s of mp3 -> 1,362,715 B in 0.006 s wall; a 30 s sub-range of AAC -> 720,431 B in 0.005 s; 15 s of HLS -> a valid `.ts` or a 38%-smaller `.mkv`. A lavfi/af tap cannot yield audio at all (the chain is single-in/single-out, no file sink), and decoded PCM at the ao is unreachable while he listens because `AudioOut::File` (`o=`/`of=wav`/`oac=pcm_s16le`, player.rs:813-819) *replaces* the device rather than teeing it.

Coexistence is measured, not assumed: `@viz:astats` (decode stage) ran simultaneously with an armed `stream-record` and two `dump-cache` calls (demuxer stage) with no interference. CPU: decode+astats alone 1.41 s over 66 s = 2.1% of one core; with the tap active 2.27 s over 95 s = 2.4%. The tap is essentially free because it is not a tap - it is a read of a buffer that is already being filled.

`stream-record`'s one surviving job: horizons longer than the cache. The cache is RAM and grows ~1:1 with retained bytes (RSS 105,220 -> 106,432 kB over 90 s), so a 3-hour KFJC show is not a `dump-cache` case. If he ever says "record this whole show", arm `stream-record` at the show's start and disarm at its end - one entry, one format, no `loadfile` in between, which dodges all three of its traps. That is a different, explicit gesture, not the mark.

Not usable: `dump-cache <start> no <file>` (continuous append mode). It only returns when playback stops or `mp.abort_async_command` fires, and `libmpv2::Mpv::command` is a synchronous `mpv_command_string` with no async/abort binding in libmpv2 4.1.0. It would wedge the actor thread that drives EOF and queue-advance, permanently. Bounded numeric-end `dump-cache` is the only reachable form without dropping to `libmpv2-sys`.

## The cut

Three rules, in this order, and the design's honesty lives in labelling which one produced a given file.

**Rule 0 - dump wide first, refine on disk.** This is the load-bearing rule and it comes from a measurement: the moment `SwitchWarmed` lands (`playlist-play-index 1` + `playlist-remove 0`, player.rs:1474-1477), 110 s of retained history vanished and `dump-cache 0 5.758549` wrote a **0-byte file while returning `"error":"success"`**. The RAM ring is volatile and destroyed by every skip and station change; a file is not. So a press never tries to be clever in the moment. It immediately (~6 ms) dumps a deliberately generous window and only then reasons about boundaries, re-cutting the local file with `ffmpeg -c copy -ss/-to` - no network, no re-encode, and repeatable as many times as he likes.

**Rule 1 - the ICY edge, where it exists.** `apply_icy_stream_meta` (handler.rs:475-537, working tree) already produces `IcyTransition::Changed` versus `NoChange`, plus `stream_title_since` and a `prev_icy: RetiredTitle { raw, retired }`. That is precisely a boundary and its instant. And it lands at the right moment: mpv holds a steady ~16.0 s `demuxer-cache-duration`, so a naive ICY apply would flip the title 16 s early, but mpv 0.29.0 shipped timed metadata for ICY (fixing issue #2453) and applies the tag when playback reaches its packets. Residual error is encoder-side plus up to one `icy-metaint` (~1 s at 128 kbps). So on Modular Station the exact cut is `dump-cache [pos_at_prev_change, pos_at_this_change]` taken at the *next* title change - a single retroactive call, no arming, and it works even if he pressed 30 s into the track. This serves Modular Station (the station that gave 43 of his 59 logged tracks), and Radar Lisboa / Oxigenio to whatever extent their ICY is track-level.

**Rule 2 - the Shazam offset, as a search window and not a cut point.** `songrec recognize --json` passes Shazam's entire envelope through untouched, and it contains `matches: [{"id":..., "offset":-6.500591796, "timeskew":..., "frequencyskew":0.0}]`. hypodj's `RecognizeResponse` (recognize.rs:347-350) declares only `track`, so serde silently drops it today. Verified live that offset advances 1:1 with wall clock (two captures 51.988 s apart, offset delta 51.6484). A **negative** offset is the strong case: it means the track began *inside* our own capture, i.e. a boundary located retroactively on material with no ICY at all. Two hard limits: there is no confidence field (six top-level keys, and Apple's `MatchedMediaItem` documents none), and it reports position in the *studio recording*, so a DJ's +4% pitch shift costs 0.04 x elapsed (0.4 s at 10 s in, 9.6 s at 240 s in), and a track dropped in at its second chorus makes `now - offset` land in the previous track's tail. So the offset narrows an unbounded search to a seconds-to-tens-of-seconds window; it does not authorise a cut. One anchoring trap that would silently corrupt every cut: `SignatureGenerator` pads anything under 12 s and *centre-crops* anything over 12 s, so slices handed to songrec must be exactly 12.000 s or the offset re-anchors to the file midpoint.

**What I refuse to build, on measurement.** Content-based boundary detection on a beatmatched mixtape. `silencedetect` found **zero** detections in 45 s of NTS 2 down to -30 dBFS with d=0.3 s - not few, none. `aspectralstats` flux is available free alongside `@viz` (both `af-metadata/viz` and `af-metadata/nov` read back simultaneously, ~0.3% of a core), but on that same capture it exceeded 10x median in 73 of 1938 frames at 0.60, 0.63, 0.86, 0.88 s - those are beats, and beatmatching is by definition the alignment of those onsets across the transition. Flux is an onset detector, not a structural one, and ffmpeg ships no self-similarity or novelty-kernel filter. Building a boundary detector here would be building something that fires where it is not needed and is silent where it is.

**Per station, plainly.** Modular Station: exact, free, already timestamped. NTS 1 / Moon Mission: show-level ICY only, so the cut is a show heading plus a marked moment, never a track edge. **NTS mixtapes (his main listening): no exact cut exists.** What he gets is a generous, honestly-labelled window - `[press_pos - 360 s, press_pos + 120 s]` by default - optionally narrowed by an offset when Shazam hits, which the study puts at 15-25%. Lot Radio (HLS): the cut mechanics work identically (dumps contain audio only, since `vid=no` means only the audio stream is demuxed), but there is no title signal, so it behaves like a mixtape. Cannot serve at all: anything across a skip or station change, and the first seconds after any load.

## Cost

**Network: negative.** This is the only capture design that adds zero bytes. It also lets `recognize.rs` delete its second HTTP fetch - today 0.17 s CPU / 9.73 s wall / 401 KB per call becomes a ~350 KB `dump-cache` in ~6 ms - and it removes the HLS live-edge offset the design doc flagged as real and unmeasured, because the window comes from mpv's own read position instead of a fresh connection with its own `-live_start_index`.

**CPU: measured, 2.1% -> 2.4% of one core.** Per-dump cost is bandwidth-bound at ~350 MB/s of page cache, so 8 minutes of NTS mp3 (32.2 KB/s -> ~15.5 MB) is ~45 ms. That is the number that matters, because it is 45 ms of *blocked actor thread* (see risks), not 45 ms of CPU spent somewhere harmless.

**RAM: already being paid, and its ceiling is unproven.** Retained bytes track RSS ~1:1; NTS mp3 retains 32.2 KB/s (~115 MB/h), Modular Station and Lot Radio's audio ~25 KB/s. I tried to pin it and failed twice. The ground reader found `--demuxer-max-back-bytes=1MiB` did not bound retention (full 119 s still dumpable). I then tried `--demuxer-donate-buffer=no --demuxer-max-back-bytes=2MiB` and got a **new and more precise fact**: after 97 s, `total-bytes=1,015,792` (the packet cache *is* bounded, ~32 s) but `file-cache-bytes=3,160,192` and `seekable-ranges=[(0.0, 97.31)]` with `bof-cached=true`. So `dump-cache` is served by the stream-level byte cache, which `demuxer-max-back-bytes` does not govern (except at 0, which disables retention entirely). The plausible real ceiling is `--demuxer-max-bytes` (150 MiB default ~= 80 minutes of NTS mp3), untested at scale. A design shipping on this must pin `demuxer-max-bytes` explicitly at init (player.rs:774-823, where the six existing properties are set - note it sets *no* cache options today) and then watch RSS in production rather than trust a knob I could not make bite.

**Disk: only on a press.** An 8-minute NTS segment is ~15.5 MB; five marks an evening is ~78 MB; a month of that is ~2 GB. Context that constrains the budget: `/` is **91% full, 91G free**, single LUKS filesystem for everything, and the offline store is at 2.20 GiB with an 8 GiB cap it is still actively backfilling toward (~5.8 GiB of headroom it intends to eat). So a tape budget cannot be generous by default. `--cache-on-disk=yes` would move the ring to disk and bound RAM, and I am explicitly rejecting it: it writes continuously, forever, on a 91%-full disk and on battery, to buy a bound I can get by capping `demuxer-max-bytes` instead.

**Battery: net improvement.** No new network at all, and it removes a wifi-radio wake of ~11 s per recognize call. Disk writes happen only on a press.

**Contention: not with the filters, only with the thread.** astats is decode-stage and the tap is demuxer-stage; measured coexisting. The store's reconciler is a separate task doing all its I/O in `spawn_blocking`, so it shares only the disk. The one real contention is that `mpv.command` blocks the actor thread that drives TimePos, EOF and queue-advance.

**One adjacent waste worth naming because it dwarfs everything above:** Lot Radio's `index.m3u8` is a master playlist with five video variants up to 1080p @ 5.2 Mbps, and `--hls-bitrate` defaults to `max`, so mpv pulls 613,863-1,221,251 B/s to retain ~25 KB/s of audio. He is paying that today, independent of this feature.

## What is kept

**Only what he marks. Nothing else ever touches disk.** The ring is RAM inside mpv and costs nothing extra because it already exists; that is exactly why this design can afford to keep nothing by default.

This is not a footprint argument, it is the design doc's own evidence turned into a storage policy: exhaustive-and-bare converted 0 of 24 radio names; chosen-and-pointer converted 3 of 7. An always-on archive across 22 stations at 25-32 KB/s is ~100 MB/hour/station and produces more of the artifact he provably abandoned in January 2026 while still listening. Gesture-only persistence is the shape his own record endorses.

**Where it lives, and where it absolutely must not.** A sibling under the state dir, `~/.local/state/hypodj/tape/`, beside `resume.toml` and `store/`. This is not a preference; `config.rs:59-65` already documents the rule for the heard ledger and the store enforces it structurally: `scan_dir` classifies every file in the store root and anything without a valid sidecar becomes `DeleteFile` on the next full pass (at most 900 s away, immediately at startup), and `remove_audio(suffix=None)` sweeps every file sharing an `<id>.` prefix. `scan_dir` is non-recursive and skips subdirectories entirely, so a sibling directory is invisible to it.

**The store's eviction is not reusable and I am not pretending otherwise.** Every input is server-authoritative: `total_bytes` sums index entries, `pinned_now` needs `getStarred2`, and `commit` hard-requires `song.size > 0` from the server with a sidecar whose `endpoint` must literally be `"download"`. A radio segment has no SongId the server knows, no size it can report, and no pin verdict - it cannot enter the index or be scored by that LRU, and it cannot be committed through that path without falsifying provenance. So `tape` gets its own trivial sweep: oldest-first by press time, skipping anything he has flagged keep, under a `[tape].max_bytes` I would default to 2 GiB (about four months of five-marks-an-evening) with a per-segment duration cap of ~20 minutes so one runaway press cannot eat the budget.

**What *is* reused is the shape, not the code:** `atomic_write_bytes` (resume.rs:143 - sibling temp, fsync, rename, already shared by store and resume), the `tmp.<pid>.<seq>` unique-temp naming, sidecar-commits-last, and the one-owner-task + `spawn_blocking` discipline. This matters more here than usual because *mpv* writes the dump file from its own thread, outside our control - so the daemon must let mpv write to `tmp.<pid>.<seq>`, then stat it, then rename, then write the sidecar. Sidecar last means a half-written dump is an orphan the next sweep removes, never a segment the UI offers.

**Config follows the house pattern exactly** (config.rs): `#[serde(default)]` field on `Config`, per-knob `#[serde(default = "d_tape_*")]` with `pub const DEFAULT_TAPE_*`, a **manual** `impl Default` (never derived - the file documents at :101 and :224 why, and there are parity tests at :1335 and :1353 asserting missing-section == empty-section), a total `normalize()` that clamps up and never rejects, and `dir: Option<PathBuf>` resolved in main.rs with the `.or_else(|| state_dir.map(|d| d.join("tape")))` shape at main.rs:293-304, warning and running without the feature if neither resolves.

## What it is for

**Not a library entry, and the store makes that structural rather than a matter of taste.** A radio rip has no MBID, no reliable tags, and is not the studio master; `store::commit` would refuse it. So: **a wantlist item with the audio attached**, plus a permanent input to deferred identification.

Concretely, the next morning: `dj heard` already renders marks first and separate. A marked row now carries a duration, a cut-method label, and a playable path. He runs `dj tape play 3` - the segment is a local file and the player already loads local store paths, so this is `loadfile` on a path, not new machinery - and hears the thing he half-remembers instead of trying to reconstruct it from a timestamp. Then one of three things happens:

1. **He recognises it himself.** The row becomes a wantlist line with artist/title he typed, the URL and time still attached. He keeps or drops the audio.
2. **Deferred identification resolves it.** This is the capability that only exists because the audio was kept. Live Shazam gets one 11 s shot at a moving target; an 8-minute file on disk can be fingerprinted from any position, any number of times, with no further fetch of the *stream*. The scarce resource is Shazam calls (IP-keyed limiter, and a block hits his whole machine), not audio - so the budget is small and deliberate: at most two extra slices per segment, each exactly 12.000 s to keep the offset anchored at file start, chosen at points the live attempt did not cover. On a stream where the live 15-25% hit rate applies to one throw, three well-placed throws at a stationary target is a materially different number.
3. **Nothing resolves it.** Then it stays as a pointer *with the sound in it* - station name, stream URL, wall-clock time, cut method, and eight minutes of audio. That is strictly the artifact that converted for him before (the Lot Radio -> six Takuya Nakamura albums path was a pointer he could act on), with the one thing that pointer lacked: he can check it is the right moment before spending an evening chasing it.

The sidecar beside each segment records station name, stream URL, wall clock, the entry-relative `time-pos` range that produced it, ICY title if any, and `cut: icy-edge | shazam-offset | uncut-margin`. That last field is the whole honesty of the feature. `icy-edge` means this is the track. `uncut-margin` means this file certainly contains what you heard and also contains other things. Labelling that difference is what stops a mixtape segment from quietly pretending to be a track.

## Composition

**With the mark gesture: this is the missing half, and it is the specific wall the design doc named.** Its own words: the press is structurally late, "on an ICY stream that is fixable by marking the previous title. On a mixtape it is not, without a continuously running PCM ring." The ring exists, it is not PCM, and it is already running. `mark_decision` (heard.rs:618) stays exactly as it is - pure, total, unchanged. The tape hangs off its *result*: `MarkSubject::Icy` and `Previous` get an exact cut from `stream_title_since` / `prev_icy.retired`; `MarkSubject::Moment` - the case the doc calls "honestly UNSOLVED" - gets an `uncut-margin` segment, which is precisely the case where audio is worth more than any label. `MarkSubject::Song` gets nothing; he already owns it. One new `HeardRow` field (a segment path) and the ledger is unchanged otherwise.

**One timebase discipline that must land before anything else, or the whole thing is subtly wrong.** `dump-cache` indexes the stream's own timeline, the same one `time-pos` and `seekable-ranges` report. Every boundary the daemon currently knows is stamped in wall clock: `stream_title_since: Option<(QueueId, Instant)>` (handler.rs:234), `prev_icy.retired: Instant`. Across a pause, an underrun, or a buffering stall those two clocks diverge, and a wall-clock boundary converted to a cache offset by subtraction lands in the wrong place. The actor already observes `time-pos` (player.rs:1033, property id 0), so the fix is cheap and must be done first: carry the entry-relative playback position *alongside* the `Instant` on every boundary, and drive cuts from the position.

**With recognize.rs: it deletes the second fetch outright.** `capture_and_recognize` currently runs `ffmpeg -i <url> -t 11 ...` - a full side-band re-download of a stream already being decoded, 65 bytes pulled per byte of fingerprint sent. `dump-cache` replaces it with a local extraction, then one `ffmpeg -i <local> -ac 1 -ar 16000` transcode of already-owned bytes. The existing `TempFileGuard` RAII, `kill_on_drop(true)`, null stdin and `RECOGNIZE_TIMEOUT` all survive intact - only the input source changes. Two second-order wins: HLS windows stop being offset by a fresh connection's `-live_start_index`, and Lot Radio's tokenised `?tkn=` URLs never need re-requesting, because we never re-request anything. And widening `RecognizeResponse` (recognize.rs:347-350) to keep `matches[]` is a struct field, not a new subsystem - the offset is already arriving on stdout.

**With store.rs: strictly beside it, never inside it.** Different root, different budget, different (much simpler) eviction, same discipline - unique temp name, verify, rename, sidecar last. The one shared resource is the 91G of free disk, and the tape budget has to be set knowing the store still intends to grow by ~5.8 GiB.

**With the warm-prefetch machinery:** measured clean. During a warm window (`playlist-count=2`) `dump-cache` describes only the audible entry - a 10 s dump gave exactly 321,160 B of station A - so a tap can never accidentally capture the prefetched entry. The instant `SwitchWarmed` lands, the old history is gone. Rule 0 (dump wide immediately) is what makes that survivable.

## Weakest point

On the streams he actually listens to most, this design does not cut - it crops. The measurements say so without ambiguity: zero silence detections in 45 s of NTS 2 down to -30 dBFS, and spectral flux firing 73 times in those 45 s on beats that beatmatching aligns straight through the transition. There is no ICY line to change and no content signal to find. So for an NTS mixtape the honest deliverable is "an 8-minute file that certainly contains what you heard", occasionally narrowed by a Shazam offset that arrives maybe one time in five and drifts with the DJ's pitch fader. Everywhere the cut is exact - Modular Station - he already had a perfect free tracklist and used to paste it by hand. The capture is genuinely retroactive, genuinely free, and genuinely removes the duplicate fetch; the *intelligent boundary detection* in the ask is the part I am delivering least of, precisely where he needs it most, and I would rather say that than ship a flux-threshold detector that produces confident cuts on the beat.

The second weakest point is narrower but would bite in production: I tried twice to bound the RAM ring and failed both times, and my better measurement only proved that the obvious knob governs a different pool than the one dump-cache reads. Until `demuxer-max-bytes` is pinned and RSS is watched across a real multi-hour evening, "the ring is free because it already exists" is true about CPU and network and unverified about memory.
